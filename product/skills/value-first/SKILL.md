description: Review websites, product UX, READMEs, and promotional materials for compelling value, brand appeal, trust, and low cognitive load. Use when the user asks for value-first or a review of value proposition, hooks, branding, or product communication.
---

Review the artifact without editing it. Maximize perceived value and trust. Minimize the effort to understand, decide, and succeed.

Identify the audience, desired outcome, supported product advantage, and next useful action from the artifact and available context. Ask only when missing information would materially change the review.

## Value proposition and hook

Does the opening make clear who this is for, what they can achieve, and why they should care? Does a memorable hook retain enough literal clarity to understand the offer?

Look for one dominant value proposition supported by relevant capabilities and evidence. Flag feature inventories, implementation details, maker effort, and generic praise that obscure the customer's outcome. A concrete alternative to “An innovative, all-in-one productivity platform” is “Turn meeting notes into assigned tasks,” when supported by the product.

## Brand appeal

Do voice, typography, imagery, spacing, and motion form a coherent hierarchy, make the benefit tangible, and create a memorable identity? Preserve useful personality and delight; an empty, generic surface can reduce both clarity and appeal.

Use Toss and Apple as cues for approachable confidence, focused storytelling, and product-led presentation. Judge fit with this audience and identity; do not recommend copying their slogans or appearance. Respect the established brand unless a redesign is requested.

## UI/UX: visual noise and hierarchy

Apply aesthetic and minimalist design: reduce visual noise so the product's value, evidence, and primary action stand out. Judge visual hierarchy, brand specificity, and usability rather than how sparse or fashionable the screen looks.

Check for generic AI aesthetics, including predictable gradients and cookie-cutter components:

- Gradients, glows, glass effects, and shadows that compete with content or weaken contrast.
- Excessive or indiscriminate border-radius and pill styling that blur distinctions between controls, content, and decoration or conflict with the brand.
- Nested cards, redundant borders, decorative icons, and badges that fragment related information or give everything equal emphasis.
- Oversized heroes, empty spacing, and repeated feature grids that displace product evidence or make scanning and comparison harder.
- Gratuitous animation, hover effects, and scroll reveals that distract, delay access, or obscure interaction states.

Treat these as inspection cues, not proof of AI authorship or automatic defects. For each finding, explain how the treatment weakens value communication, brand identity, trust, or task completion. Recommend removing, reducing, or consolidating the treatment while preserving useful affordances and brand expression. Do not impose flat design, sharp corners, or a different fashionable template as the cure.

## Cognitive load and progressive disclosure

AI tends to fill space, show everything, and explain every choice. Judge information by its audience value, not its availability.

- Flag repetition, decorative claims, redundant labels, and creator-focused explanations that do not help readers recognize value, assess fit, trust, or act.
- Recommend progressive disclosure for secondary detail near the question it answers, farther down the page, or behind a clearly named link or control. Recommend removing information with no audience value rather than hiding it.
- Keep decision-critical costs, conditions, limitations, and consequences visible before commitment. Preserve navigation, accessibility, feedback, and error recovery; fewer words or controls can create more work.
- Check that the primary next action is clear and labeled by its outcome, with useful alternatives subordinate. Avoid prescribing a universal hero, cards, FAQ, and CTA template.

For a website, check the promise, product or result demonstration, and next step. For a README, check purpose, fit, a short reliable path to first success, discoverable prerequisites, and links to deeper reference. Judge the audience's journey rather than section count.

## Trust and first success

Are claims supported nearby by working examples, product views, verifiable results, or attributable testimonials? Flag inflated superlatives, artificial urgency, misleading ease, and promises stronger than their evidence. State material uncertainty; never invent metrics, endorsements, customers, guarantees, or capabilities, including in suggested copy.

Inspect the rendered surface and relevant journey when available; check README first-use instructions against the product. Can the audience understand the benefit, believe it, choose confidently, and reach first success without unnecessary reading or steps? Does the presentation remain distinctive and appealing?

Report findings in the user's language, prioritized by impact. For each, identify the location, evidence, audience consequence, and smallest useful improvement; offer sample copy when it clarifies the recommendation. Do not default to adding content or manufacture findings. Say when no useful change is evident. State validation limits and do not claim user testing or conversion gains without evidence.
